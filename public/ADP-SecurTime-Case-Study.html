Project Overview

A Legacy Product, Reimagined Entirely

SecurTime is ADP's enterprise time and attendance solution. Years of accumulated UX debt left the interface dense and inconsistent. I was tasked with a complete ground-up redesign across all user roles.

🎯

The Problem

The legacy interface lacked visual hierarchy, had no clear user journeys, and used inconsistent patterns. Employees and managers struggled to complete basic tasks efficiently.

🧭

My Approach

Followed ADP's 5D process — Discover, Define, Develop, Design, Deliver — anchored in 20-participant user research and the Waypoint Design System throughout.

✦

The Outcome

A modern, scalable UI serving three distinct user roles within one cohesive system. Clear hierarchy, predictable navigation, and dashboard-first architecture across all flows.

🗂️

Modules Redesigned

Dashboard, Punch In/Out, Absence Management, Time & Attendance, Timesheet, Kiosk Management, Reports, Settings, Calendar, Team Management, Shift Management, Bulk Upload, Help Center.

👥

User Personas

Three user roles shaped every decision: Employee (Lisa Morgan), Manager (Nitesh), and Admin (Ritesh). Shared patterns ensure visual consistency; role-specific flows address distinct goals.

🔧

Methods & Tools

Figma hi-fi prototyping, ADP Waypoint DS, heuristic evaluation of legacy product, moderated usability sessions, HEART framework evaluation, and iterative stakeholder reviews.

Design Rationale

Key Decisions And Why They Were Made

Every pattern was deliberate. These are the most impactful UX decisions — and the reasoning behind each.

01

Dashboard-First Architecture

The most frequent action — Punch In/Out — was elevated to a persistent CTA on every screen. The dashboard surfaces today's work hours, shift, leave balance, and requests at a single glance. Daily task depth reduced from 4+ clicks to 1.

Reduced daily task friction
02

Dual Dashboard for Managers

Managers need both a team view and personal view. Rather than separate pages, a prominent toggle card switches between "Team Dashboard" (approval hub + team stats) and "My Dashboard" (personal tasks). One URL, two contexts — no navigation debt.

Team View + Personal View in one
03

Progressive Disclosure for Punch

Punch information is surfaced inline on the dashboard in an expandable section. A confirmation modal auto-fills time, date, and location — only notes require input. This reduces errors and cognitive load significantly.

Auto-populated confirmation modal
04

Semantic Status Hierarchy

All statuses — Approved, Rejected, Pending, Submitted, Withdrawn, Draft — use a consistent colour-coded pill system applied uniformly across Absence, Attendance, Requests, Subscriptions, and Bulk Upload tables.

Consistent across 8+ modules
05

Tabs to Manage Complexity

Absence Management has 5 request types; Settings has 4 areas; Shift Management has 4 sub-views. Tabs within pages reduce the sidebar from 20+ items to 5 top-level nodes — a 75% reduction in navigation depth.

Navigation depth reduced ~75%
06

Team Insights with Real Data

Managers needed trend visibility without exporting reports. The Team Insights section provides Attendance, Leave, Punctuality, and Overtime charts (Week/Month toggle) with Peak/Lowest/Average KPIs — making data-driven team management possible inline.

Attendance, Leave, Punctuality, OT trends
07

Admin System Health Panel

Admins needed a real-time view of device and system status without a separate IT dashboard. The System Health panel surfaces Active Devices (3/5), Devices with Issues (7), and Failed Transactions (20) — plus a Policy & Compliance panel for violations, OT, and late marks.

System Health + Policy Compliance
08

In-Product Help Center

Rather than linking to external documentation, a Help Center module lives within the product with User Guide and FAQ sections, filterable by role (All / Employee / Manager / HR / Admin). A sticky Contents sidebar and category navigation enable fast self-service support.

Role-filtered in-product documentation

Results & Reflection

What This Redesign Delivered

From a dense legacy product to a clean, role-aware enterprise application — a ground-up redesign covering the full breadth of ADP's time and attendance platform.

User pain points uncovered & directly addressed by the redesign

Enterprise UX Demands Clarity Over Cleverness

Enterprise products serve users across every technical comfort level. Good design here means clear hierarchy, forgiving flows, and always-visible status. Every pattern was tested against: "Would a first-day employee understand this?"

Design Systems Accelerate Quality

Using Waypoint from day one meant focusing on interaction and IA decisions rather than micro-visual ones. Every component already solved accessibility, spacing, and responsiveness — freeing me to focus on the problems that actually mattered.

Multiple Personas Sharpen Decisions

Having three distinct personas forced concrete trade-offs: what does the employee need here vs. the admin? These tensions surface better decisions than designing for a generic "user" — and the personas evolved throughout the process.
